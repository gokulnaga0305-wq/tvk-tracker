# Is Tamil Nadu a "drug state"? — A sourced briefing

*A fact-check briefing for outreach. Every figure is sourced. The framing is
deliberately even-handed — it concedes the real problem too, so it holds up to
scrutiny. Feed this to NotebookLM to generate an audio overview / FAQ / etc.*

## The claim being checked
"Tamil Nadu has become a drug state — drug trafficking is rampant under the
[previous DMK] government."

## The one-line verdict
**No.** The Government of India's own survey shows Tamil Nadu is among the
**lowest** states for actual drug **use**. TN ranks high only on **enforcement**
(seizures and cases) — which measures how hard the police work, not how many
people use drugs.

## 1. The core trick: catching drugs ≠ using drugs
The whole "drug state" narrative is built by quoting **enforcement numbers**
(seizures, NDPS cases, arrests) and treating them as if they measure **use**.
They don't. If a town hires more police and catches more thieves, that means
better policing — not more thieves. NCRB analysts say the same: a rising case
count "cannot be attributed to a rise in crime" — it can simply reflect more
reporting / more policing.

## 2. PREVALENCE — what the Government of India's survey actually shows
Source: **National Survey on Extent and Pattern of Substance Use in India, 2019**
(AIIMS-NDDTC / Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment).
- **Children's drug use (current):** Tamil Nadu ~**0.37%** vs India **1.17%** —
  far below average. The high states are Arunachal (8.65%), Sikkim, Delhi (7.85%).
- **People who inject drugs (PWID):** TN is **NOT in the top 10**. The leaders are
  UP, Punjab, Delhi, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Haryana, Karnataka, Maharashtra.
- **Cannabis, opioids, sedatives, cocaine, ATS, hallucinogens:** TN appears in
  **no** "top-states" prevalence list. Where TN shows up, it's only in
  absolute-count tables (a population artifact — TN is a large state).

## 3. ENFORCEMENT — where TN does rank high (and why)
TN's NDPS cases/seizures are high because the state **cracked down**, not because
use is high. The decisive tell:
- **Arrests rose** (≈14,394 → 17,903) **while ganja seized FELL** (28,383 →
  21,424 kg) over 2022–24. If TN were flooding with drugs, seizures would rise,
  not fall. (TN police / Enforcement Bureau-CID, via The Federal, Outlook, The
  South First.)
- TN's NIB-CID head states TN has **zero local ganja cultivation** — supply comes
  from **Andhra Pradesh and Odisha** (which seized ~1.4–1.7 lakh kg in a year vs
  TN's far smaller volume). TN is a **transit/consumer** state, not a producer.
- *Caveat:* TN's 2023–24 NCRB tables are unpublished, so recent state figures are
  police briefings, not audited NCRB data. Treat them as enforcement output.

## 4. What the government actually did about drugs (2021–26)
- Created a dedicated drug-enforcement bureau (EB-CID, 2022).
- "Drug-Free Tamil Nadu" — anti-drug clubs on campuses; ~30 lakh student pledge.
- Anti-Narcotics Intelligence Unit (2024): ~90 networks dismantled, 583 arrests.
- Cannabis crops worth ~₹4,000 cr destroyed; assets frozen.

## 5. The honest concession — TN is NOT drug-free
This matters for credibility — concede it plainly:
- **Synthetic / pharma "party pills" are surging** (narcotic tablets +255% in 2024)
  and **youth are over-represented** — 1 in 3 caught peddling them in Chennai are
  under 25; police busted a student-run meth lab (Kodungaiyur, 2024).
- The problem is **concentrated** at coastal smuggling points (Ramanathapuram →
  Sri Lanka) and a few college belts (Coimbatore, Chennai) — not statewide.
- The point is not "zero problem." It's that **TN uses less than most states**, the
  drugs mostly **pass through** rather than grow here, and the government cracked
  down hard.

## The bottom line
Tamil Nadu ranks **high on catching drugs** and **low on using them.** Calling
that "a drug state" confuses the **crackdown for the crime.**
