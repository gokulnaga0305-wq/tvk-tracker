# Does Tamil Nadu "run on" TASMAC / liquor money? — A sourced briefing

*A fact-check briefing for outreach. Every figure is sourced. The framing is
deliberately even-handed — it states the counter-arguments too, so it holds up
to scrutiny. Feed this to NotebookLM to generate an audio overview / FAQ / etc.*

## The claim being checked
"The Tamil Nadu government mainly runs on TASMAC / liquor income."

## The one-line verdict
**Misleading.** The ₹48,344 crore everyone quotes is TASMAC's **sales turnover**, not
government income. What the treasury actually earns from liquor is about **a quarter of
its own-tax revenue** — the single largest individual revenue stream, but **nowhere near
a majority.** TN does **not** "run on" liquor.

## 1. The core trick: turnover ≠ government income
Three different numbers get mixed up:
- **₹48,344 cr (FY25)** = TASMAC **sales turnover** — the total value of every bottle
  sold. It includes the maker's cost, the taxes, and the shop margin. It is NOT govt income.
- **₹48,344 cr = ₹37,324 cr VAT + ₹11,020 cr excise** — what the treasury actually books
  from liquor in FY25.
- **~25% of own-tax revenue / ~16% of total revenue** — liquor's real share of state income.

TN collects most of its liquor money as **VAT (sales tax), not excise** — so its
"state excise" line looks tiny next to states like UP. You must **add VAT + excise** to
see the real take. Opponents quote gross turnover (inflates it); some defenders quote
excise-only (deflates it). The truth is in the middle.

## 2. Where the state's money really comes from (FY24 own-tax, approx)
- SGST: ~₹66,967 cr
- **Liquor (VAT + excise): ~₹46,000 cr**
- Petrol/diesel VAT: ~₹30,000 cr
- Stamp duty & registration: ~₹25,567 cr
- Other (motor vehicle, etc.): ~₹22,000 cr

SGST + fuel tax + stamp duty together are ~two-thirds of own-tax revenue. **Liquor is
big — the biggest single line — but it is one slice, not the engine.**

## 3. Share of the whole budget & economy (the zoom-out)
- ~**25%** of own-tax revenue (what TN raises itself)
- ~**11%** of the total state budget (₹4.39 lakh cr expenditure, 2025-26)
- ~**1.5%** of Tamil Nadu's whole economy (GSDP ~₹31 lakh cr)

A meaningful slice — **not** the thing the state "runs on."

## 4. Tamil Nadu isn't special — it's mid-pack among states
Liquor as a share of each state's **own-tax revenue** (RBI bands):
- **More dependent than TN (>20%):** Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, Uttarakhand
- **Around TN (15–20%):** Tamil Nadu ~15%, Punjab, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, Telangana, Himachal
- **Most other states:** 10–15%

So "TN runs on liquor" would have to be said about most of India first. The other
extreme — prohibition: **Bihar and Gujarat earn ~₹0** from liquor (Bihar forgoes an
estimated ₹4,000–8,000 cr/yr) **and still see recurring hooch deaths.** Banning the
revenue didn't ban the drinking.

## 5. Why TN's "excise" looks deceptively low (the VAT shift)
A viral infographic ranks states by **excise duty only** (% of GSDP) and puts TN
2nd-from-bottom at 0.4%. True but a trap: TN taxes liquor mostly through **VAT**, not
excise. Add TN's liquor VAT (~₹35,000 cr ≈ 1.1% of GSDP) and TN jumps from **0.4% → ~1.5%**
of GSDP — up near Karnataka/AP, not at the bottom. The 0.4% is real but misleading.

## 6. "Drinking is a personal choice"
True — the government doesn't create the demand and doesn't invite anyone to drink.
But it **isn't a neutral bystander** either: it is the **monopoly seller** and has raised
prices to grow the take. It doesn't manufacture the demand, but it **does monetise and
depend on it.**

## 7. The ₹10-per-bottle — it's a refundable deposit (with a catch)
Commonly misread as a simple "overcharge." Actually:
- Since 2025 the extra ₹10 is officially a **refundable deposit** — pay ₹10, return the
  empty bottle, get ₹10 back. The minister called it a "deposit." It backs a **court-driven
  empty-bottle buyback** the Madras HC has ordered since ~2023 (to stop bottles being
  reused for spurious liquor).
- The catch: the buyback **failed to take off** — the Madras HC (June 2026) rapped TASMAC
  ("no licence to collect extra money" unless refunded) and TASMAC is rolling out
  deposit-return machines. So when the ₹10 isn't returned, it works like overcharging.
- Separately, TASMAC has faced above-MRP overcharging complaints (9,319 cases, FY20).
  The viral "₹15 crore a day" figure is an unaudited opposition extrapolation.

## 8. The honest steelman (so it can't be ambushed)
The strongest version of the OTHER side, stated fairly:
- Liquor is the **single largest individual revenue stream** — roughly tying SGST.
- It's about **a quarter of own-tax revenue** — losing it would blow a hole no easy
  substitute fills, which is exactly why no TN govt seriously moves to prohibition.
- It **doubled in a decade** (₹25,846 cr FY16 → ₹48,344 cr FY25), partly via price hikes.
- It is **regressive** — the burden falls heaviest on poor and working-class households.

**Defensible truth:** TN is structurally dependent on a very large, regressive,
single-source liquor revenue (~a quarter of own-tax income) it has every reason to grow —
but it is **false** that the state "mainly runs" on it.
